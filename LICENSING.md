## Agent-generated development

The initial implementation was generated by AI coding agents under human
direction, with agent-executed automated checks. Much of the accompanying
tests and documentation was also agent-generated. Later contributions may
have different provenance. This describes the development process, not the
identity, personal circumstances, or location of any contributor.

This disclosure is not a representation that every line was human-authored,
manually audited, or independently cleared for third-party rights. It does
not identify an AI agent as a legal author or copyright owner, or assert
exclusive human copyright over every generated portion.

Provider terms can govern permission to use generated output, but contractual
permission does not itself establish copyrightability or remove third-party
rights. Applicable provider terms and the provenance of any copied or adapted
material still need to be checked before distribution. This project has not
undergone a comprehensive legal/provenance clearance review.

## Copyright and enforceability

Copyrightability depends on applicable law and the nature of the actual
contributions. Qualifying original human expression may be required; time
spent, functional requirements, or approval of generated code do not by
themselves establish protection. Original human-authored design material
or other expressive contributions may matter even when a tool produces an
implementation. Ideas, functionality, and technical constraints should not
be confused with protectable expression.

Not typing the code does not alone decide authorship, but prompting,
testing, reviewing, or approving output does not automatically establish
copyright in the generated implementation. The status of particular
agent-generated portions and any human-authored selection, arrangement,
design material, or modifications is fact-specific. This project makes no
blanket determination that all generated code is protected or public domain.

An MPL declaration cannot create missing copyright. Consequently,
copyright-based copyleft enforcement cannot be assumed for portions lacking
qualifying protection. Development records can help establish actual
contributions; timestamps or deposits alone do not create copyright. If
enforceable reciprocity is important, seek qualified software/IP legal advice
for the applicable jurisdictions. This explanation is general information,
not a legal determination of authorship, ownership, or enforceability.

## Publication privacy

Do not disclose private information about maintainers, contributors, device
owners, or their installations in project text, examples, fixtures, logs,
diagnostics, package metadata, or distribution artifacts. This includes
personal names or account handles, private contact details, geographic
location, home-directory paths, real network addresses, and device identifiers.
Use relative paths, synthetic fixtures, and reserved example addresses rather
than copying local configuration, captures, or private development records.
Preserve legally required third-party notices; do not invent personal
attribution for generated material.

Review Git author/committer metadata, repository URLs, generated metadata,
and archive headers before publishing: editing working files does not remove
information from existing history or previously generated artifacts. History
rewrites and attribution changes require an explicit, coordinated decision.
The source-distribution configuration uses generic archive ownership rather
than the build account. This is packaging metadata, not author attribution.
